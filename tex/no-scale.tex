  \begin{abstract}
    Version~1 of this note claimed a Level~1 rigidity theorem: no dimensional parameter independent of the
    Born\textendash Infeld constant~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ can enter the admissibility constraints of the Cosmochrony
    programme, with a corollary excluding Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om and Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter
    exteriors.
    The present version withdraws that claim, its corollary and their application.
    The proof rested on premises that the cited papers do not contain (a power law for the pair capacity, a
    continuum limit, a fibre-weight requirement, an axiom fixing a unique primitive scale), and its three cases
    have internal gaps.
    The failure of the proof does not show that every Level~1 property is false: the question is open.
    What remains is dimensional.
    The objects of the model fix only relations between dimensions, not the physical dimension
    of~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, and the corpus reads that constant inconsistently; this gap is recorded rather than
    filled.
    The definition of an independent dimensional parameter is therefore stated relative to a supplied dimensional
    assignment.
    Under such an assignment, and if the bound's right-hand side is a quantity of the amplitude's dimension, a
    constant factor that depends only on a parameter whose dimension lies outside the span of the dimensions of
    the bound's ingredients cannot make the bound depend on that parameter; a dimensionless factor rescales
    $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    The Level~1 / Level~2 distinction is kept, and both levels are open.
    The reading that physical scales should arise as outputs of the projection rather than as primitive inputs is
    labelled as an interpretation, not a result.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    admissibility; dimensional analysis; dimensional homogeneity; Born\textendash Infeld bound; non-injective
    projection; scale rigidity; open problem; emergent physics

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

    Version~1 of this note stated a theorem (``Level~1''): any deformation of the admissibility constraints of
    the Cosmochrony programme that introduces a dimensional parameter independent of the constant
    $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ breaks spectral scaling, structural non-injectivity or bounded admissibility closure.
    A corollary excluded any dynamical field with an independent dimensional coupling, and a remark applied it to
    the Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om and Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter exteriors.

    An audit of the note against the current versions of its sources found that the proof does not establish
    the statement.
    The present version corrects the status of the note.
    Three things are kept apart throughout.
    \begin{enumerate}[(1)]
      \item \emph{The proof is invalid.}
        Its premises are not found in the cited papers, and each of its cases has an internal gap
        (Section~\ref{sec:withdrawn}).
      \item \emph{The claim is withdrawn.}
        The note no longer asserts the Level~1 theorem, its corollary, or the exclusion of
        Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om and Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter exteriors.
      \item \emph{The question is open.}
        An invalid proof does not show that its conclusion is false.
        Nothing in this note shows that an independent dimensional parameter can be introduced consistently into
        the admissibility constraints, and nothing shows in general that it cannot.
    \end{enumerate}
    What remains established is a set of dimensional observations, stated in
    Sections~\ref{sec:framework} and~\ref{sec:homogeneity} with their premises.

  \section{Framework, attributions and the dimension of \texorpdfstring{$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$}{c\_BI}}
  \label{sec:framework}

  \subsection{What the sources state}

    Version~1 attributed the bound and a unique primitive scale to the axioms A3 and A4 of the Foundation
    paper~\cite{Beau2026m}.
    The attribution is corrected as follows.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item In Foundation, A3 is \emph{admissibility as non-premature selection} and A4 is \emph{projection
        locking}.
        Both refer to the saturation threshold of the capacity axiom [A-cap] below, but neither states the
        mode-amplitude bound~\eqref{eq:bound} or a single-scale condition, and neither excludes a dimensional
        parameter.
        The amplitude bound appears among the internal constraints listed in A1, and at the level of the
        admissibility filter~$\Pi_n$.
      \item The load-bearing input is the capacity axiom [A-cap] of~\cite{Beau2026c}: a uniform bound
        $|\partial_\tau\phi_v|\le c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ on the per-node relaxation rate.
        The Born\textendash Infeld form is the saturation candidate compatible with [A-cap]; its unique selection
        is open.
      \item The mode-amplitude bound
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:bound}
          A_n \;\le\; \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}
        \end{equation}
        used below is stated as a hypothesis (``[H2]'') in~\cite{Beau2026a24}.
        Here $\lambda_n$ is the Laplacian eigenvalue of the mode and $A_n$ its amplitude.
      \item No source cited by version~1 states that $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ is \emph{the unique primitive
        dimensional scale}.
        The single-scale premise of version~1 was assumed, not imported; papers that repeated it from version~1
        are corrected in the same cycle as this version.
      \item No axiom of Foundation is a power-law constraint on the pair capacity $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$.
    \end{itemize}

  \subsection{The dimension of \texorpdfstring{$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$}{c\_BI} is not fixed by the model}
  \label{subsec:dimension}

    A statement about independent dimensional parameters needs the dimension of~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    We record what the objects of the model define.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item In [A-cap], $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]=[\phi]\,[\tau]^{-1}$.
        The variable $\phi$ is a relational configuration on the nodes of a weighted graph, and $\tau$ is the
        monotonic ordering parameter of the relaxation.
        Neither is assigned a physical dimension, and~\cite{Beau2026c} states that [A-cap] does not fix the
        relation between $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and any continuum scale.
      \item In~\cite{Beau2026a24}, the $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ of~\eqref{eq:bound} is the bound of [A-cap], and the
        envelope is obtained for an oscillatory mode of frequency $\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ by applying [A-cap] to the
        modal rate, $A_n\sqrt{\lambda_n}\le c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
        That derivation is conditional on [A-cap] and on the existence of a second-order oscillatory sector.
        Under it, $[A_n]=[\phi]$ and $[\lambda_n]=[\tau]^{-2}$: the eigenvalue is a pure number only if the
        ordering parameter is.
      \item The white paper~\cite{Cosmochrony} names $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ the ``fundamental admissible projective
        speed'' and the ``maximal admissible flux speed'', and reads the effective speed of light as its
        projection.
        [A-cap] does not import that reading.
    \end{itemize}
    The model therefore fixes relations between dimensions,
    $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]=[\phi][\tau]^{-1}$, $[A_n]=[\phi]$, $[\lambda_n]=[\tau]^{-2}$ (the last two conditionally), but
    not the dimensions $[\phi]$ and $[\tau]$ themselves, so the physical dimension of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ is
    \emph{not fixed}.
    The white paper's reading of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ as a speed is not part of the current axiom, and the corpus is
    inconsistent on this point.
    This is a gap in the framework.
    The present note does not choose a dimension to make the definition below applicable.

  \subsection{Independent dimensional parameters, relative to an assignment}

    A \emph{dimensional assignment} is a choice of physical dimensions for the objects of the model.
    We write a dimension as an exponent vector over a set of base dimensions, so that the dimensions form a
    $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space and $[x^a y^b]=a[x]+b[y]$~\cite{Buckingham1914,Bridgman1922}.

    \begin{definition}[Independent dimensional parameter]
      \label{def:independent-scale}
      Fix a dimensional assignment in which $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ has a dimension.
      A parameter $\lambda$ is an \emph{independent dimensional parameter} if $[\lambda]$ is not a rational
      multiple of $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$, that is, $[\lambda]\neq k\,[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$ for every
      $k\in\mathbb{Q}$.
      A dimensionless parameter ($[\lambda]=0$) is not independent in this sense.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{remark}[Scope of the definition]
      \label{rem:definition-scope}
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item Because the model fixes no assignment (Section~\ref{subsec:dimension}),
          Definition~\ref{def:independent-scale} is a scheme, not yet a criterion that can be applied to the
          programme.
        \item Under any assignment in which $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ carries a single dimension, the definition makes
          independent every quantity whose dimension is not a multiple of it.
          In a description with independent base dimensions of length, time and mass, at most one direction of
          the dimension space is a multiple of $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$, so the gravitational constant~$G$, the
          Planck constant~$\hbar$ and any mass scale cannot all be reduced to $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
          A no-scale statement of the form of version~1 would therefore exclude them too, unless they were
          shown to be dimensionless combinations or outputs of the projection.
        \item A finite resolution parameter, such as the prime $q$ of the finite
          Heisenberg carrier, is a pure number.
          Version~1 added that it disappears in the $q\to\infty$ continuum limit.
          That limit is not established: the published admissibility form converges to the zero form, and a
          normalisation no-go excludes a non-trivial toric differential operator from a common scalar
          normalisation~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.
          The remark is withdrawn; $q$ is simply dimensionless.
        \item Definition~\ref{def:independent-scale} measures independence against the multiples of
          $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$ alone.
          This is adequate only when the spectral data are dimensionless.
          If, as in the relations of Section~\ref{subsec:dimension}, $[\lambda_n]$ is not a multiple of
          $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$, the definition makes the model's own spectral parameter independent, and a Level~1
          statement worded with it would fail trivially.
          Independence would then have to be measured against the span of the dimensions of the model's own
          quantities, as in Observation~\ref{obs:homogeneity}.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{remark}

  \section{An observation on dimensional homogeneity}
  \label{sec:homogeneity}

    The only part of the version~1 proof that survives is its case~(a), a global constant factor.
    It survives as an observation about the bound~\eqref{eq:bound}, not as a theorem about the admissibility
    structure.

    Consider the modified bound
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:deformed-bound}
      A_n \;\le\; \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}\,f(\lambda),
    \end{equation}
    with $f$ a function of a single positive parameter~$\lambda$, independent of the depth~$n$ and of the
    configuration.

    \begin{observation}[Dimensional homogeneity of a constant factor]
      \label{obs:homogeneity}
      Fix a dimensional assignment in which~\eqref{eq:bound} is homogeneous, and suppose that the right-hand side
      of~\eqref{eq:deformed-bound}, the admissible maximum $A_n^{\max}$, is a quantity of dimension $[A_n]$: its
      numerical value transforms as $[A_n]$ under every change of units of that assignment.
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item If $[\lambda]$ does not lie in the $\mathbb{Q}$-span of $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$ and $[\lambda_n]$, then
          $f$ is constant.
          The factor carries no dependence on~$\lambda$, and $\lambda$ does not enter the bound.
        \item If $\lambda$ is dimensionless, $f(\lambda)$ is a dimensionless constant and is absorbed by the
          rescaling $c_{\mathrm{BI}}\mapsto c_{\mathrm{BI}}\,f(\lambda)$.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{observation}

    \begin{proof}
      (i) The dimensions of $A_n$, $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and $\lambda_n$ span a subspace~$W$, and $[A_n]\in W$ by
      homogeneity of~\eqref{eq:bound}.
      Since $[\lambda]\notin W$, there is a linear form $u$ on the dimension space with $u(W)=0$ and
      $u([\lambda])=a\neq 0$.
      Rescaling the units by $s^{u}$, $s>0$, leaves the numerical values of $A_n$, $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and
      $\lambda_n$ unchanged and sends $\lambda$ to $s^{a}\lambda$.
      Since $u([A_n])=0$, the numerical value of $A_n^{\max}$ is unchanged by this rescaling, while the
      right-hand side becomes $c_{\mathrm{BI}}\lambda_n^{-1/2}f(s^{a}\lambda)$.
      Hence $f(\lambda)=f(s^{a}\lambda)$ for all $s>0$, so $f$ is constant on $(0,\infty)$.
      The hypothesis on the right-hand side is needed: the mere truth of~\eqref{eq:deformed-bound} in every
      system of units does not force this, since for $f(\lambda)=1+\lambda$ the deformed bound holds whenever
      \eqref{eq:bound} does.
      This is the covariance argument behind the $\pi$ theorem~\cite{Buckingham1914,Bridgman1922}.
      (ii) $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ enters~\eqref{eq:bound} linearly, so the factor is absorbed by
      $c_{\mathrm{BI}}\mapsto c_{\mathrm{BI}}\,f(\lambda)$.
      Version~1 wrote $f(\lambda)^{1/2}$; that was an error.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[What the observation does not exclude]
      \label{rem:homogeneity-limits}
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item The observation needs a dimensional assignment, which the model does not supply
          (Section~\ref{subsec:dimension}).
        \item A parameter that is independent in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:independent-scale} but whose
          dimension lies in the span of $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$ and $[\lambda_n]$ can enter homogeneously through an
          $n$-dependent combination such as $\lambda/\lambda_n^{k}$.
          If the shell data carry dimensions, further combinations exist.
          Dimensional homogeneity alone does not exclude these.
        \item The observation concerns the bound~\eqref{eq:bound} only.
          It says nothing about scales elsewhere in the programme, and nothing about depth-dependent or
          configuration-dependent factors.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{remark}

  \section{The withdrawn Level 1 claim}
  \label{sec:withdrawn}

  \subsection{What version 1 claimed}

    Version~1 stated, as Theorem~3.1, that
    \emph{any deformation of the bound by a factor $\Phi(\lambda;\mathrm{data}_n)$, in the form
    $A_n\le c_{\mathrm{BI}}\lambda_n^{-1/2}\Phi(\lambda;\mathrm{data}_n)$, that introduces an independent dimensional parameter breaks at least one of
    (i) BFS-consistent spectral scaling of $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$, (ii) structural non-injectivity and the
    ENI no-go theorem, (iii) bounded admissibility closure.}
    Here $\mathrm{data}_n$ denotes the BFS-level data at depth~$n$ (shell size, spectral weights, Weil block
    structure).
    The proof divided $\Phi$ into a constant factor (case~a), a depth-dependent factor (case~b) and a
    configuration-dependent factor (case~c).
    A corollary excluded dynamical fields with independent dimensional couplings.

  \subsection{Why the proof does not establish it}

    \paragraph{Premises not found in the cited papers.}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{Axioms.}
        The bound and a unique primitive scale were attributed to A3 and A4 of Foundation, which state neither
        (Section~\ref{sec:framework}).
      \item \emph{A power law for the pair capacity.}
        Case~(b) relied on~\cite{Beau2026a28} for a power-law scaling of
        $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)=\sigma_c(n)\,\sigma_{q-c}(n)$, ``determined uniquely'' by the admissibility
        structure.
        That paper proves a verticality condition and the stability of the observable rank under supplied
        carrier and fibre hypotheses.
        It states no power law for $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ determined by the admissibility structure; it
        reports a finite-window fitted exponent at one prime as requiring confirmation.
      \item \emph{A continuum limit.}
        Case~(b) concluded that disrupting the power law ``destroys the existence of the continuum limit'' by a
        Mosco convergence result attributed to~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.
        That paper proves the opposite kind of statement: the published admissibility form converges to the zero
        form, and the spatial continuum question is left open.
        There is no established limit for a deformation to destroy.
      \item \emph{A fibre-weight requirement.}
        Case~(c) attributed to the ENI theorem~\cite{Beau2026l} the requirement that configurations with the
        same observable image receive identical admissibility weights.
        ENI concerns surjective projections whose observables are defined by~$\Pi$: injectivity is equivalent
        to structural isomorphism, so genuine emergence forces non-injectivity and strict information loss.
        It states no constraint on weights over a fibre.
    \end{itemize}

    \paragraph{Internal gaps.}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{Case (b).}
        Even granting a power law, and if the factor transferred multiplicatively to $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$, a
        factor bounded uniformly in~$n$ would change $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ by a bounded factor and leave the
        exponent unchanged.
        An unbounded factor need not change it either: $n^{-\delta}\log n$ keeps the exponent~$\delta$.
        No law relating a deformation of the filter to the deformation of $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ was given.
      \item \emph{Case (c).}
        A weight $\Phi(\lambda,\omega)$ on the configurations does not change~$\Pi$: configurations with equal
        images keep equal images, and $\Pi$ stays non-injective.
        The weight is a further function on the configuration space, not an injectivisation of~$\Pi$.
        Excluding it needs a premise that admissibility weights are constant on fibres.
        No paper of the programme states that premise.
        Positing it would exclude fibre-dependent weights only because the premise says so.
      \item \emph{Exhaustiveness.}
        Combinations of the three cases were not treated, and a dependence of $\Phi$ on shell data other than
        the depth falls in none of them.
      \item \emph{Corollary.}
        It used the premise that any dynamical contribution enters the bound through its energy or action.
        That premise was not derived.
        It also placed every dimensionless coupling in case~(a), which holds only for a constant factor.
    \end{itemize}

    \paragraph{The Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om / Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter application.}
    Version~1 counted $[\Lambda]=[\mathrm{length}]^{-2}$ as independent of~$[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$.
    If $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$ were a non-zero power of a length, $\Lambda$ would not be independent under
    Definition~\ref{def:independent-scale}.
    If it were not, the Schwarzschild radius $r_s=2GM/c^2$, a length, would be excluded on the same ground, together
    with the Schwarzschild exterior the application was meant to single out.
    The remark also treated the Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om charge, an integration constant of the solution
    like the mass, as a coupling constant.
    The application is withdrawn.
    Version~1 also attributed to the companion paper Q6b a Schwarzschild uniqueness result and a ``Remark~3.3''
    relying on this note.
    The current version of Q6b~\cite{Beau2026q6b} contains neither: it makes no uniqueness statement, and it
    records that the geometric sector already carries independent scales.
    That record is quoted here as corroboration only; the withdrawal rests on the internal inconsistency above.

  \subsection{Status after the correction}
  \label{subsec:status}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{Invalid proof.}
        The gaps above show that the version~1 argument does not establish Theorem~3.1 or its corollary.
      \item \emph{Withdrawn claims.}
        The Level~1 theorem, its corollary and the Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om /
        Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter application are withdrawn.
        Version~1's statements that the Gram rigidity classification is derived under the bound and is
        stabilised by Level~1 are withdrawn as well (Section~\ref{sec:gram}).
      \item \emph{Open question.}
        Whether some Level~1 property holds is open.
        A precise formulation would need, at least, a dimensional assignment for the objects of the model, an
        exact class of deformations, and a proved relation between a deformation of the filter and the
        observables it affects.
        Two directions are recorded as open problems, not as results:
        \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
          \item which depth-dependent deformations of the filter change the behaviour of
            $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ at large depth, and by what law;
          \item whether any principle of the programme imposes fibre-constant admissibility weights.
        \end{enumerate}
        No conditional theorem is stated in their place.
        A conditional statement would be retained only with an exact formulation, a proved implication, and
        content beyond its own hypotheses.
    \end{itemize}

  \section{Level 1 and Level 2}
  \label{sec:discussion}

    The distinction of version~1 between two levels is kept.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{Level~1 (no independent parameter):} no dimensional parameter independent of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$
        enters the admissibility constraints.
      \item \emph{Level~2 (functional determination):} every quantity of the admissibility cascade is computable
        from $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and the spectral structure, without free parameters or unfixed initial
        conditions.
    \end{itemize}
    Level~2 is intended as the stronger property; it implies Level~1 when the spectral data are dimensionless
    (Remark~\ref{rem:definition-scope}(iv)).
    Both are open.
    Level~2 would require, among other things, showing that exponents such as $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ and
    $\beta^*$ are determined by the BFS geometry.
    These exponents are dimensionless, so Level~1 alone would not constrain them.

  \section{The Gram rigidity classification}
  \label{sec:gram}

    Version~1 stated that the Gram rigidity paper~\cite{Beau2026a3} derives its classification under the bound
    $A_n\le c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$, and that Level~1 makes the classification structurally fixed.
    Both statements are withdrawn.
    The theorem of~\cite{Beau2026a3} concerns a finite group $G$, a symmetric generating set $S$ and an
    irreducible representation $\rho\colon G\to\mathrm{SU}(2)$ with $\chi_\rho(s)=0$ on $S$.
    It identifies $\rho(G)$ as $\mathrm{Dic}_n$ ($n\ge 3$), $Q_8$, $2O$ or $2I$, and excludes $2T$, by character
    theory.
    No admissibility bound and no dimensional parameter enters its hypotheses.
    The classification is scale-free and needs no stability statement.

  \section{Interpretation}
  \label{sec:interpretation}

    \emph{The following paragraph is an interpretation, not a result of this note.}
    The programme's working reading is that physical scales should arise as outputs of the projection and of the
    spectral structure rather than as primitive inputs.
    On that reading, an emergent scale such as a mass ratio or a spectral threshold is not an ``external''
    parameter, even if its dimension differs from that of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    This reading is a guideline for model building.
    It does not follow from Observation~\ref{obs:homogeneity}, which concerns one bound under a supplied
    assignment, and it is not supported by a proved Level~1 statement.
    The geometric sector already carries scales that are not derived from $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$~\cite{Beau2026q6b}.

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

    This version withdraws the Level~1 theorem of version~1, its corollary and its application to
    Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om and Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter exteriors.
    The proof rested on premises absent from the cited papers and had internal gaps in each case.
    That failure does not settle the question, which remains open.
    The note keeps three dimensional points.
    \begin{enumerate}[(1)]
      \item The model fixes relations between dimensions but not the physical dimension of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, and
        the corpus reads it inconsistently, so any statement about independent dimensional parameters needs a
        supplied dimensional assignment.
      \item Under such an assignment, and if the bound's right-hand side is a quantity of the amplitude's
        dimension, a constant factor depending only on a parameter whose dimension lies outside the span of the
        bound's ingredients cannot make the bound depend on that parameter, and a dimensionless factor rescales
        $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ (Observation~\ref{obs:homogeneity}).
      \item Level~1 and Level~2 are distinct, and both are open.
    \end{enumerate}
    The reading that scales are outputs rather than inputs remains an interpretation
    (Section~\ref{sec:interpretation}).
