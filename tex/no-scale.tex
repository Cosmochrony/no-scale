  \begin{abstract}
    The admissibility structure of Foundation~M~\cite{Beau2026m} is built on a single primitive dimensional
    scale~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ entering the Born\textendash Infeld bound (A3\textendash A4).
    We establish a structural rigidity result (Level~1): any deformation of the admissibility
    constraints that introduces a dimensional parameter independent of~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ breaks at least
    one of BFS-consistent spectral scaling, structural non-injectivity (A2/ENI),
    or bounded admissibility closure (A3\textendash A4).
    As an immediate corollary, dynamical fields carrying an independent dimensional coupling
    cannot be consistently coupled to the projection~$\Pi$; this provides the structural
    underpinning for the exclusion of Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om and
    Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter geometries invoked in the companion paper~Q6b.
    A stronger Level~2 statement, asserting full functional determination of all emergent scales,
    is beyond the scope of the present note and is identified as an open problem.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    admissibility; dimensional analysis; scale rigidity; non-injective projection; Born–Infeld constraint; spectral
    scaling; Mosco convergence; structural rigidity; emergent physics; admissibility cascade; effective scale

  \section{Introduction and Motivation}
  \label{sec:intro}

  \subsection*{Guiding principle}

    The admissibility structure of the Cosmochrony programme~\cite{Cosmochrony} is closed under
    reparameterisations of the primitive scale~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, but not under the introduction of
    independent dimensional parameters.
    The present note makes this principle precise.

  \subsection*{Motivation}

    The companion paper Q6b establishes that the effective spacetime geometry derived from the
    admissibility operator $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ selects the Schwarzschild metric as the unique
    exterior stationary solution compatible with spherical symmetry and bounded admissibility.
    The exclusion of the Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om and Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter
    deformations rests on the claim that no dimensional parameter independent of~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ enters
    the admissibility structure: a charge $Q$ with dimensions $[\mathrm{charge}]$ or a cosmological
    constant $\Lambda$ with dimensions $[\mathrm{length}]^{-2}$ would each introduce such a parameter.
    The present note supplies the structural backing for this claim.

  \subsection*{Scope and limitations}

    The result proved here (Level~1) establishes that no independent dimensional parameter can be
    introduced into the admissibility constraints without breaking structural consistency.
    It does not establish Level~2: that every emergent scale is uniquely determined as a functional
    of~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and the cascade, without free parameters.
    The distinction is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

  \section{Framework and Definitions}
  \label{sec:framework}

  We recall the admissibility axioms of Foundation~M~\cite{Beau2026m} in the form needed for
  the present argument.
  Throughout, $G_q = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ is the discrete Heisenberg group
  at prime $q$, $\{S_n\}$ is the BFS shell stratification, $\lambda_n$ is the spectral quantity
  associated to the $n$-th admissibility window, and $A_n$ is the admissibility amplitude.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item[(A2)] The projection $\Pi\colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{O}$ is non-injective; distinct
    underlying configurations may share the same observable image.
    \item[(A3)] The Born\textendash Infeld admissibility bound holds:
    $A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}} / \sqrt{\lambda_n}$, where $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ is the unique primitive dimensional
    scale of the admissibility structure.
    \item[(A4)] The admissibility closure is bounded: the projected spectral capacity
    $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) = \sigma_c(n)\cdot\sigma_{q-c}(n)$ on conjugate Weil
    blocks satisfies the power-law constraint established in~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
  \end{itemize}

  \begin{definition}[Independent dimensional parameter]
    \label{def:independent-scale}
    A parameter $\lambda$ is called an \emph{independent dimensional parameter} (with respect to
    the admissibility structure) if the dimensions of $\lambda$ are not expressible as a rational
    power of the dimensions of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    Equivalently, $[\lambda] \neq [c_{\mathrm{BI}}]^k$ for any $k \in \mathbb{Q}$.

    A \emph{dimensionless} parameter (i.e., $[\lambda] = 1$) is not an independent dimensional
    parameter in this sense.
    In particular, any parameter entering the admissibility structure only through a dimensionless
    ratio with~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ (i.e., through $\lambda/c_{\mathrm{BI}}^k$ for some $k \in \mathbb{Q}$) is not
    independent in the sense of this definition, and reduces to a rescaling of~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    Finite resolution parameters such as $q \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ are dimensionless and disappear
    in the $q \to \infty$ continuum limit; they are excluded from the scope of the present definition.
  \end{definition}

  \begin{definition}[Deformation of the admissibility bound]
    \label{def:deformation}
    A \emph{deformation} of the admissibility bound is the replacement
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:deformed-bound}
      A_n \;\leq\; \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}\,\Phi(\lambda;\,\mathrm{data}_n),
    \end{equation}
    where $\lambda$ is an additional parameter (possibly dimensional) and $\mathrm{data}_n$
    denotes the BFS-level data at depth~$n$ (shell size, spectral weights, Weil block structure).
    The function $\Phi$ is assumed measurable and bounded away from zero and infinity on compact sets.
  \end{definition}

  \section{Main Result}
  \label{sec:main}

  \begin{theorem}[No external dimensional scale, Level~1]
    \label{thm:no-external-scale-L1}
    Let the admissibility structure of Foundation~M be given with primitive dimensional scale~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    Any deformation of the admissibility constraints in the sense of
    Definition~\ref{def:deformation} that introduces an independent dimensional parameter $\lambda$
    (in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:independent-scale}) breaks at least one of the following:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item BFS-consistent spectral scaling of $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$,
        \item structural non-injectivity (A2) and the ENI no-go theorem~\cite{Beau2026l},
        \item bounded admissibility closure (A3\textendash A4).
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    We analyse exhaustively all structural forms that the deformation factor $\Phi$ can take.
    Since $\Phi$ depends on $\lambda$ and $\mathrm{data}_n$, there are three cases according to
    the nature of this dependence.

    \paragraph{Case (a): global multiplicative deformation.}
      Suppose $\Phi = f(\lambda)$ is independent of $n$ and of the configuration~$\omega$ in
      the fibre.

      If $\lambda$ is an independent dimensional parameter, then $[\lambda]$ is incommensurable with
      $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]$, so $f(\lambda)$ must carry non-trivial dimensions to yield a dimensionally homogeneous
      right-hand side in~\eqref{eq:deformed-bound}.
      But $A_n$ and $c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ are objects of the same dimensional type by construction
      of the admissibility bound.
      Introducing $f(\lambda)$ with $[\,f(\lambda)\,] \neq 1$ produces a right-hand side of different
      dimensional type, making the bound ill-typed.
      This violates the closure of A3.

      If $\lambda$ is dimensionless, then $f(\lambda)$ is a dimensionless constant and can be absorbed
      into a redefinition $c_{\mathrm{BI}} \mapsto c_{\mathrm{BI}}\,f(\lambda)^{1/2}$, producing no new scale.
      The deformation is trivial in this case.

      Thus no non-trivial global multiplicative deformation by an independent dimensional parameter
      is admissible.

    \paragraph{Case (b): depth-dependent deformation.}
      Suppose $\Phi = \Phi(\lambda, n)$ depends on the BFS depth~$n$.

      The combinatorial growth of BFS shells $|S_n|$ is a property of the Cayley graph
      $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$ and is not directly modified by the admissibility bound.
      However, the pair-level projected observable
      $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) = \sigma_c(n)\cdot\sigma_{q-c}(n)$ records the effective spectral
      capacity selected by the admissibility filter at depth~$n$.
      The paper~\cite{Beau2026a28} establishes that $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ follows a power-law
      scaling compatible with the Mosco convergence of Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a}.

      A depth-dependent factor $\Phi(\lambda, n)$ introduces an additional $n$-dependence in the
      admissibility selection at each BFS shell.
      More precisely, the weight assigned to each conjugate Weil block $(c, q-c)$ at depth~$n$ is
      modulated by $\Phi(\lambda, n)$, altering the asymptotic behavior of $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$.
      Since the power-law scaling of $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ is determined uniquely by the
      admissibility structure with scale~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and the BFS geometry (established in~\cite{Beau2026a28}),
      any deformation that is not a trivial $n$-independent rescaling (hence falls back to Case~(a))
      breaks this scaling.
      By the Mosco convergence result of Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a}, disrupting the power-law scaling of
      $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ destroys the existence of the continuum limit, violating closure (i).

    \paragraph{Case (c): configuration-dependent deformation.}
      Suppose $\Phi(\lambda, \omega)$ depends on the configuration $\omega$
      in the projection fibre above a given observable.

      A configuration-dependent admissibility filter assigns different selection weights to
      configurations within the same fibre, i.e., to configurations that share the same observable
      image under $\Pi$.
      This means that observable-equivalence classes --- the projection fibres --- are no longer
      defined by $\Pi$ alone; the admissibility filter introduces a finer discrimination within each
      fibre.
      In other words, $\Phi(\lambda,\omega)$ induces a partial injectivisation: configurations
      $\omega, \omega'$ with $\Pi(\omega) = \Pi(\omega')$ but $\Phi(\lambda,\omega) \neq
    \Phi(\lambda,\omega')$ are no longer genuinely indistinguishable at the admissibility level.
      This contradicts the structural non-injectivity of A2 and the ENI theorem~\cite{Beau2026l},
      which requires that distinct underlying configurations sharing the same observable image be
      assigned identical admissibility weights.

    \paragraph{Conclusion.}
      Every deformation by an independent dimensional parameter $\lambda$ falls into one of Cases~(a),
      (b), or~(c), or a combination thereof.
      In each case the deformation either reduces to a trivial redefinition of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ or violates
      at least one of (i), (ii), (iii).
      This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:no-external-scale-L1}.
  \end{proof}

  \section{Corollary: No Additional Field Without Scale}
  \label{sec:corollary}

  \begin{corollary}[No additional field without independent dimensional scale]
    \label{cor:no-field-without-scale}
    Under the admissibility structure of Foundation~M~\cite{Beau2026m}, any additional dynamical
    field coupled to the projection $\Pi$ and carrying an independent dimensional coupling constant
    violates Theorem~\ref{thm:no-external-scale-L1}.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{proof}
    Let $F$ be a dynamical field carrying a dimensional coupling constant $g_F$ with
    $[g_F] \neq [c_{\mathrm{BI}}]^k$ for any $k \in \mathbb{Q}$.
    Any dynamical contribution to the admissibility structure must enter the Born\textendash Infeld
    bound through its energy or action contribution, and therefore modifies the right-hand side
    of~A3.
    Coupling $F$ to the admissibility structure thus introduces $g_F$ into the right-hand side
    of~A3 as an independent dimensional factor.
    By Definition~\ref{def:independent-scale}, $g_F$ is an independent dimensional parameter, and
    by Theorem~\ref{thm:no-external-scale-L1} the resulting deformation breaks closure.

    Note that fields with \emph{dimensionless} coupling constants (e.g., topological phases,
    certain geometric angles) are not excluded by the present argument: their coupling factor
    $\Phi(\lambda,\cdot)$ with $[\lambda] = 1$ falls into Case~(a) of the proof and reduces to a
    trivial rescaling of $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    Such fields do not introduce independent dimensional scales and require separate analysis.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Application to Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om and Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter]
    \label{rem:RN-SdS}
    The electromagnetic charge $Q$ in the Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om metric carries
    dimensions $[Q]$ independent of $[c_{\mathrm{BI}}]^k$ for any~$k$.
    The cosmological constant $\Lambda$ in the Schwarzschild\textendash de~Sitter metric carries
    dimensions $[\Lambda] = [\mathrm{length}]^{-2}$, likewise independent.
    By Corollary~\ref{cor:no-field-without-scale}, neither admits a consistent coupling to the
    admissibility structure of Foundation~M, which provides the structural basis for the exclusion
    argument of Remark~3.3 in the companion paper~Q6b.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Discussion and Relation to Level 2}
  \label{sec:discussion}

  Theorem~\ref{thm:no-external-scale-L1} establishes that admissibility is rigid with respect to
  the introduction of new independent dimensional scales.
  This is a \emph{negative} structural result: it rules out extensions, but does not determine
  the values of the scales that are already present.

  \subsection*{What this result does not say}

    Theorem~\ref{thm:no-external-scale-L1} does not assert that all emergent scales are
    fully determined by $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ without residual freedom.
    Specifically, it leaves open whether derived quantities such as $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$
    or $\beta^*$ --- which are of dimensional type compatible with derivation from $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ via
    the spectral cascade --- are uniquely fixed as functionals of~$c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, or whether
    they carry unfixed normalisation constants or initial conditions.
    Resolving this question is the content of Level~2.

  \subsection*{Level 2: full functional determination}

    A Level~2 statement would take the following form:
    \begin{quote}
      \emph{Every quantity of the admissibility cascade is computable from $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and the
      spectral structure of Foundation~M, without free parameters or unfixed initial conditions.}
    \end{quote}
    This is a much stronger claim.
    It requires, in particular, showing that the power-law exponent $\beta^*$ of the cascade
    is a determined functional of the BFS geometry and $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, not merely a quantity consistent
    with the scaling established in~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
    Level~2 is left as an open problem.

  \subsection*{Derived scales are admissible, not independent}

    Theorem~\ref{thm:no-external-scale-L1} does not exclude the emergence of effective scales
    from the cascade.
    Such scales (e.g., mass ratios, spectral thresholds) are admissible precisely because they
    arise as outputs of the cascade, not as independent inputs.
    The criterion of Definition~\ref{def:independent-scale} cleanly separates the two categories:
    an emergent scale $\mu$ is admissible if and only if $[\mu] = [c_{\mathrm{BI}}]^k$ for some $k$, or
    if $\mu$ is dimensionless.

  \subsection*{Stabilisation of the Gram rigidity classification}

    The Gram rigidity paper~\cite{Beau2026a3} establishes that the admissibility constraint
    with bound $A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ forces the neutral-generator structure of the
    projection to be classified by exactly four families of finite subgroups of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$:
    $Q_8$, $\mathrm{Dic}_n$ ($n \geq 3$), $2O$, and $2I$.
    This classification is derived for the admissibility window defined by $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.

    Theorem~\ref{thm:no-external-scale-L1} provides the missing stability statement: the
    admissibility window cannot be deformed by an independent dimensional parameter.
    Consequently, the Gram rigidity classification is not contingent on a particular choice of
    scale, but is structurally fixed: no deformation of the admissibility bound can produce a
    different list of compatible groups.
    The group classification is, in this sense, as rigid as the scale structure itself.
